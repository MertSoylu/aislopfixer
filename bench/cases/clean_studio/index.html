We work on the screens that other studios hand back.

Scheduling systems, clinical records, freight dashboards. Interfaces where being wrong is expensive and being pretty is not the point. Twelve years, forty-one products, two people.

Selected work

Four projects we can talk about publicly. The rest are behind hospital and freight contracts.

Meridian Freight

A dispatcher watches eighteen routes at once and cannot afford a modal. We rebuilt the console around a single persistent grid, moved every destructive action behind a hold gesture, and cut median task time from six minutes to ninety seconds. The redesign shipped in eleven weeks with no retraining programme, because the new layout matched the paper sheets dispatchers had been keeping beside their keyboards for years.

Ledger, for St Aubin

Medication reconciliation, on a ward, on a tablet, with gloves on.

Kestrel

An internal tool that three hundred people use daily and nobody has ever filed a complaint about. We consider this the best thing on the page.

Halyard Charts

Tide and depth data for small-craft navigation. The hard part was colour: the palette had to survive direct sunlight, a cracked screen protector and the red night-vision filter, so we designed it in three states and tested it on a boat.

How we actually work

We start by watching. Two days on site, no slides, no questions that begin with "wouldn't it be nice if". Most of what we end up changing was never in the brief.

We write the interface copy before the layout. If a screen cannot be explained in a sentence, the sentence is the problem, not the screen.

We ship in slices and we sit with the people using them. A design that survives contact with a Tuesday afternoon is worth more than one that survives a review.

We do not do brand work, decks, or "digital transformation". There are studios that are good at those and we are not among them.
