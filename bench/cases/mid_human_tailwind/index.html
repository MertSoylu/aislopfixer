We buy about four hundred kilos of tea a year.

That is not very much. It is roughly what one supermarket sells in a morning. We think this is the point.

Where it comes from

Four gardens, three of them in Nepal, one in the Nilgiris. We have visited all of them and we buy from the same people every year, which means when a harvest is bad we buy it anyway and sell it cheaper rather than going somewhere else. This is a slow way to run an import business and it is the only reason the tea is any good.

The Nilgiri garden is the odd one out — it is the only estate we work with that is larger than forty hectares, and we buy from a single block on the eastern slope rather than from the estate as a whole.

A note about "single origin"

The phrase has stopped meaning anything. A single origin tea can come from a thousand hectares and six hundred pickers, which is not a meaningfully different claim from a blend. We list the garden, the block and the picking date instead, because those are the three things that actually change what is in the cup.
