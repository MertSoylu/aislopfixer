Bell & Quire Commission

Sewn on cords, the way a book that will be opened twice a week has to be.

We rebind about ninety books a year and turn away rather more than that. Most of what we take is somebody's working copy — a score, a ledger, a recipe folder that has stopped closing.

What we actually do

Full leather, quarter leather, and a cloth binding we have been making the same way since 2011. We do not do gilt edges, we do not do box sets, and we will usually talk you out of marbled endpapers because they date faster than anything else on the book.

Repairs are the bulk of it. A spine that has gone at the joint is four days' work and costs less than most people expect; a text block that has been guillotined by a previous binder is not repairable at all, and we will say so.

Three we finished in March

A 1902 mill ledger

Rebacked in goat, original boards kept. The owner's grandfather ruled every column in it by hand and we were not about to trim that off.

Fourteen orchestral parts

Sewn flat so they lie open on a stand, which is the only thing a player actually asks for. Cloth in five colours so the section can find its own part in the dark of a pit — the sort of decision that never survives a template, because it only makes sense for this one job.

The bindery is above a joiner's on Sheepscar Street. If the door is open the press is free.

Commissioning

Bring the book. We will not quote from a photograph — half of what decides the price is how the text block has been trimmed, and that does not photograph.
